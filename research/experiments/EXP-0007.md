# EXP-0007 - Prefetch each layer's missed pageable experts in one call, and which pageable copies fault

**Status: done, Adopt** (opt-in `LLAMA_MOE_PREFETCH=2`, the probed prefetch;
defaults unchanged). Iteration 7, authorized and run 2026-11-01. This is
ROADMAP **R12** (one batched prefetch per layer), with **Q18**'s fault
attribution first and **R16b**'s second launch headroom folded in. Gate G
passed. H1 is falsified: the faults are each pageable expert's first read since
the load, not reads after a long gap. H2, H3 and H4 hold. Results and decision
at the end.

## Background

Under profile pinning at the default cap, EXP-0006 measured 2.1 slow expert
copies per token warm (tokens 387-1022; a slow copy's host call runs below 1.5
GB/s, a hard fault on the mapping) and 3.4-3.6 ms per token of GPU idle during
them, about 1.7 ms per slow copy. Address order had fewer (1.5-1.6 per token,
2.5-2.7 ms) though it leaves 28 points more of the bytes pageable (Q18). A
decode fault reads 64 KiB synchronously in about 175 us (Finding 9), so a
cold 576 to 840 KiB expert tensor costs 9 to 13 serial faults.

## Code (opt-in, defaults unchanged)

- `LLAMA_MOE_PREFETCH=1`: in `copy()`, after the router read-back and the plan,
  one `llama_prefetch` (`PrefetchVirtualMemory`) call per layer over the host
  ranges of the missed experts' tensors that are not fully pinned. Advisory, no
  data path change.
- `LLAMA_MOE_PREFETCH=2`: the same, but first one `QueryWorkingSetEx` call per
  layer drops the ranges whose middle page is in the process's working set.
  Added after the smoke run (below), before any timed run.
- `LLAMA_MOE_COPY_LOG=<path>`: per upload of `copy()` (one expert tensor, or n
  consecutive experts) the call number of the trace, layer, tensor, expert, n,
  bytes, pinned bytes and host time of the `llama_moe_cache_set_async` call; per
  prefetch the call number, layer, candidate and prefetched ranges and bytes,
  and time; and the prefill's large-batch uploads (`upload()`) as call 0, so
  the attribution knows which experts the prefill read. Buffered in memory,
  written at teardown.

**Smoke run (64 tokens, profile pinning, no eviction), 2026-11-01.** Output
identical with `LLAMA_MOE_PREFETCH` 1 and 2. A prefetch call over 1.81 MiB took
75 to 94 us when its pages were already in RAM (about 0.17 us per 4 KiB page)
and 600 to 990 us when it read (1.9 to 3.2 GB/s), so the call blocks until its
reads finish. Warm, profile pinning uploads about 48 MiB of pageable expert
data per token (EXP-0006: 22% of about 220 MiB), so prefetching all of it would
cost about 2 ms per token if it were all resident, against 3.4 to 3.6 ms of
slow-copy idle to win. That is why mode 2 exists. Also found: the prompt's last
layer runs one token through the cache (only its output row is kept), so the
trace starts with one call of layer 39 before the first decode pass; and most
pageable uploads next to a pinned expert share one page with it.

## Gate G (R12's falsifier, CPU only)

`prefetch_bench.py` maps the GGUF as `llama_mmap` does and copies a layer's k
missed experts (all three tensors) out of the mapping by `memmove`, with and
without one `PrefetchVirtualMemory` call over the same page-aligned ranges first.
Trials of the two methods alternate on disjoint, non-adjacent experts after
`evict.py` (cold), then the same trials with the working set emptied (standby)
and once more (resident). k = 1, 3, 8; 20 trials per method and k.

**Passes if** at k = 3 in the cold state the median trial time with the prefetch
(call plus copies) is at most 0.8 times the median without. **Otherwise R12 is
rejected** and the `-pf` arms are not run. Also reported: the median prefetch call
time over resident and standby pages, which bounds the warm in-situ cost.

## Hypotheses

**H1 (Q18, attribution)** - the pageable copies that fault are the ones whose
pages were not touched for long: the slow share of pageable uploads rises with
the passes since the expert's previous upload, and that explains why profile
pinning faults more per pageable copy than address order.

Uploads are binned by the passes since the host last read the same expert (an
upload in the decode, or the prefill's uploads at pass 0): 1-15, 16-127, 128+;
and `first`, not read by the host since the load (whose prefetch read the whole
file, perhaps evicted since), a class of its own with no known gap (tokens
3-1022, no-prefetch arms at the high headroom, pooled over the reps).
**Falsified by** (a) a slow share that falls from one gap bin to the next
(1-15, 16-127, 128+) in either `prof` or `addr` (a bin with fewer than 50
uploads is merged into its neighbour), or (b) a standardized share that
explains less than half of the gap: predicting `prof`'s slow share from
`addr`'s per-bin shares weighted by `prof`'s bin counts (`first` a stratum of
its own; bins merged until both arms have at least 50 uploads in each) must
cover at least half of (`prof` - `addr`) in observed slow share.

**H2 (R12, warm)** - with profile pinning at the default cap and the high
headroom, the probed prefetch makes tokens 387-1022 faster: `prof-pq` - `prof`
< 0 in both rounds and at most -0.5 ms per token on the mean (R12's estimate:
-0.8 to -1.7 ms). **Falsified by** a mean above -0.5 ms or a sign that differs
between rounds. The same rule is reported for `prof-pf` (prefetch everything),
which the smoke run predicts to gain less or lose (its resident cost); and for
both modes under address order. Also reported: slow copies and slow-copy idle
per token, and the GPU idle with no CUDA call running (where a blocking prefetch
call shows). The predictions in milliseconds are written after gate G, from its
resident and standby costs, before the timed runs.

**H3 (R12, cold)** - with nothing pinned and a cold start (128 tokens after the
eviction), the prefetch at least halves the fault wait per token and makes
tokens 3-126 faster, in both rounds (`pg-pf` against `pg`). The fault wait is
the slow-copy idle plus the GPU idle while no CUDA call runs: the prefetch call
moves the disk reads out of the copies into itself, so the slow-copy idle alone
would fall by construction (it is reported too). **Falsified by** either
failing in either round.

**H4 (R16b)** - at about 1 GiB less launch headroom (18944 against 19968 MiB),
the prefill takes longer and the slow copies per token rise: `prof-lo` against
`prof`, prompt eval time and slow copies per token on tokens 3-1022, higher in
both rounds. **Falsified by** either one not higher in either round.

The lower level is 1 GiB below the high one rather than the planned 2 GiB:
EXP-0006's runs at 19968 MiB already reached 1839 to 1954 MiB available
(`prof`) and 1019 to 1120 MiB (`addr`), so 2 GiB less would cross the 300 MiB
floor at which the monitor kills the run. For the same reason `addr` runs only
at the high level, and only `prof` (no prefetch) runs at the lower level.

### Where the gain comes from

The prefetch removes almost all of the slow-copy idle and pays for it in
no-API idle, the time the decode thread blocks in the prefetch call. In
`prof-pq` slow-copy idle falls by 3.49 ms per token and no-API idle rises by
0.87. The probe leaves 2.13 of the 49.34 candidate MiB per pass to prefetch.
Of the 37 prefetch blocks per pass (one per cached layer that missed), about
19.5 have no candidate (every missed expert pinned; 40 us per pass in all),
about 16 find every candidate range in the working set (median 5 us, 95 to 101
us per pass), and about 1.1 read (median 0.79 ms, 0.73 to 0.74 ms per pass, at
about 3 GB/s). The ranges it keeps are exactly the pageable first reads (3.49
per pass over tokens 387-1022 and 27.1 over 3-130; below), and the ranges it
drops do not fault: 0.01% (`prof-pq`) and 0.003% (`addr-pq`) of the pageable
uploads are slow, against 2.6% (`prof`) and 0.9% (`addr`) without a prefetch.

Mode 1 hands every candidate to the call, 49.34 MiB per pass under the profile
(2.2 ms per pass, about 0.17 us per 4 KiB page: mostly a walk over pages
already in RAM, gate G's resident cost, not disk reads) and 111.87 MiB under
address order (4.6 to 4.7 ms per pass, more than the 2.8 ms of slow-copy idle
it removes, so `addr-pf` is slower warm). Early in a run, while
many pages are cold, every prefetch arm wins: 8.5 to 11.8 ms per token over
tokens 3-130. `pg-pf` prefetched 225 MiB per pass (10.2 ms), and its fault wait
(slow-copy plus no-API idle) was 0.416 and 0.420 of `pg`'s. Part of the cost
moved rather than vanished: its fast-copy idle rose by 1.9 and 4.1 ms per token
(r1, r2), copies of prefetched pages that come from standby (gate G) and so
count as fast. All idle fell only to 0.65 and 0.71 of `pg`'s, and counting the
fast-copy rise as fault wait gives 0.49 and 0.58. Most of the gap between
`pg-pf`'s rounds (64.75 against 66.28 ms) is that term.

### Which copies fault (Q18, H1)

A first read is an upload of an expert that the host has not read since the
load (no upload in the decode, and none in the prefill's large batches); a
re-read is any later upload. Pageable expert uploads per rep, window named:

|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `prof` | 3-1022 | 82740 | 8601 (10.4%) | 3803, 3746 | 44% | 74139 | 3, 0 |
| `addr` | 3-1022 | 191628 | 7723 (4.0%) | 3451, 3460 | 45% | 183905 | 4, 2 |
| `prof-lo` | 3-1022 | 82740 | 8601 | 5127, 5054 | 59% | 74139 | 5, 2 |
| `pg` | 3-126 | 45405 | 6804 (15.0%) | 2084, 2068 | 31% | 38601 | 0, 0 |

The slow share of first reads depends on the window (pooled over the reps):
`prof` 33.8% over tokens 3-130 and 60.1% over 387-1022, `addr` 36.4% and
70.6%, `prof-lo` 45.6% and 78.9%; `pg` 30.5% over 3-126. And it depends on
where the expert sits in the file (below), so it is not a per-read probability.

- **The faults are first reads.** 99.9% of the slow pageable uploads are first
  reads, in every arm without a prefetch. A re-read is essentially never slow:
  0 to 5 per run among 38,600 to 184,000, at 0.70 to 1.48 GB/s (most just under
  the 1.5 GB/s rule), none after a gap of 500 passes or more.
- **Three speeds, three states** (`prof_r1`, median host rate): re-reads 7.2
  GB/s, fast first reads 3.2 GB/s, slow first reads 0.38 GB/s. A fast first
  read costs about 0.7 us per 4 KiB page more than a re-read, as a copy from
  standby does in gate G (0.9 to 1.0 us), and a slow one runs near gate G's
  cold rate (0.44 GB/s). So a re-read comes from the working set, a fast first read
  from standby, a slow one from disk. The mode-2 probe confirms the first part:
  the ranges it found outside the working set are exactly the first reads that
  were not fully pinned, in every one of `prof-pq_r1`'s prefetch calls over
  tokens 387-1022 (3.49 per pass; 27.1 over 3-130).
- **Warm, the slow copies per token are the slow first reads.** Pageable first
  reads fall from 27 per pass over tokens 3-130 to 3.5 over 387-1022 under the
  profile (32 to 2.3 under address order). Over 387-1022, `prof` has 2.07 and
  2.13 slow first reads per pass and 2.07 and 2.13 slow copies per token.

  grew from 12.9 GiB after the load to 17.3 GiB at the end, available memory
  fell from 6.4 to 2.1 GiB, and the other processes stayed flat. Level 4:
  Windows repurposes the oldest standby pages first, so the load's earliest
  pages go first; a page that a decode read maps into the working set is not
  trimmed at this headroom.
- **Why the profile faults more per pageable copy (H1 (b)).** It pins the
  experts selected most, so more of its pageable uploads are first reads
  (10.4% against 4.0% over tokens 3-1022). Standardizing on first reads and gap
  bins explains 103% of the difference in slow share there, where the two
  orders' first reads happen to be slow equally often (44%, 45%). Over tokens
  387-1022 the same standardization gives 126%: the profile's pageable first
  reads include middle layers the front has not reached, so they are less often
  slow (60% against 71%). Per token, the profile makes more slow
  copies warm (2.10 against 1.60, 3.51 against 2.81 ms of slow-copy idle)
  because it makes more pageable first reads per pass (3.49 against 2.26),
  though its token is faster overall (41.77 against 45.16 ms).
- **EXP-0006's level 4 explanation was wrong in its mechanism.** It said the
  cold tail's pages leave RAM between uses. They do not: once read, an expert
  stays. What faults is the first read of an expert whose pages were never
  resident (the file's tail) or have left standby since the load (the head).
- **A lever this points at (level 4, not measured).** The non-expert tensors
  uploaded to the GPU (2.0 GiB, 2039 MiB; the 0.5 GiB token embedding stays in
  a CPU mapping) are read through the mapping to upload them, and they stay
  mapped after the load: the loader unmaps only the parts of the file before
  the first and after the last tensor kept in a CPU mapping (here about 0.5
  GiB, `output.weight`), and on Windows `llama_mmap::unmap_fragment` does
  nothing at all (level 2, code). So those pages were probably faulted into
  the working set, where nothing trims them at this headroom: about as much
  RAM as the 2.1 GiB tail that never stayed resident. ROADMAP R21.

### Hypotheses

- **H1: falsified** by its part (a). The gap bins hold 1 to 3 slow uploads each
  among 26,000 to 219,000 (pooled over the two reps), shares of 1e-5 to 4e-5 that
  are not monotone (`prof` falls from 1-15 to 16-127). Part (b) passes only
  through the first-read stratum. What faults is not the long gap but the first
  read (above).
- **H2: holds.** `prof-pq` - `prof` -2.59 ms (-2.60, -2.57), past the -0.5 ms
  threshold, and larger than predicted (above). By the same rule `prof-pf` (-0.96) and `addr-pq` (-1.74) pass, and
  `addr-pf` (+2.03) fails, as predicted.
- **H3: holds.** `pg-pf` is 12.91 ms per token faster (-13.79, -12.04), and its
  fault wait is 0.416 and 0.420 of `pg`'s. The slow-copy idle alone falls to
  0.001 to 0.003 of `pg`'s, by construction. The rule does not count the
  fast-copy idle, which rose by 1.9 and 4.1 ms (copies from standby); with it,
  the ratios are 0.49 and 0.58, so "halves the fault wait" does not mean
  halves the stall.
- **H4: holds.** At 1 GiB less headroom the prefill took 3.25 and 3.22 s
  against 2.74 and 2.59 s (+0.52, +0.64), and slow copies per token over tokens
  3-1022 rose from 3.70 to 5.00 (+1.30, +1.28). Warm, `prof-lo` is 0.93 ms per
  token slower (+1.18, +0.67), with 1.20 ms more slow-copy idle. This is one
  further level, 1 GiB apart, so the slope is not established (R16b).

## Decision: Adopt, opt-in

Keep `LLAMA_MOE_PREFETCH` as an opt-in and recommend **mode 2 with pinning**:
-6.2% warm and -9.7% over tokens 3-1022 under the profile, -3.9% and -7.3%
under address order, the same sign in both rounds, output unchanged. Mode 1
stays (mode 2 falls back to it where the working-set probe does not exist, or
if `QueryWorkingSetEx` fails, which the copy log would show), but is not
recommended: it is slower than mode 2 in every arm and window measured, and
slower than no prefetch under address order warm.

Defaults stay as they are, because mode 2 has not run without pinning (the
cold arms ran mode 1 only), at 15000 MiB, on other prompts or other hosts. Off
Windows the probe does nothing and `llama_prefetch` is `madvise(MADV_WILLNEED)`,
which does not block; nothing there was measured.

What is left: the blocking prefetch calls cost 0.87 ms per token warm under the
profile and 3.1 ms over tokens 3-130, and they only read experts that the
decode has not read since the load and whose pages are gone: the file's tail,
which never stayed resident, and the head, lost in file order. Two levers
follow (ROADMAP): read those experts ahead of need, off the decode thread (R20;
bounded by the RAM budget, since the 18.3 GiB of experts and the process do not
fit in what is available here), and release the mapped pages of the tensors
uploaded to the GPU at load (2.0 GiB), about as much RAM as the missing tail
(R21).

## Limits

One model, one prompt, two reps in one session, a 2048 MiB cache, two
headroom levels 1 GiB apart, one host (Windows 11, WDDM). Mode 2 ran only at
the high headroom and only pinned; `prof-lo`'s attribution (re-reads slow 5 and
2 times in 74,139) says the probe's premise holds at the lower level, but the
size of its gain there was not measured. The probe looks at one page per range,
so a range half in the working set is judged by its middle page. Experts in a
pinned host buffer that this cache did not register (`--load-mode none`) are
prefetch candidates too, which wastes work; not exercised here. The copy log
adds a timer per upload (on in every arm, so the pairs are fair; its cost alone
was not measured) and is kept in memory until teardown. The fault
classification uses the timeline's rule (below 1.5 GB/s over the host call),
not a page-fault counter. In `analysis.md` and `runs.json` the `pg` arms'
windows are labelled 3-130 and 3-1022 but hold tokens 3-126, the tokens a
128-token run has.

## Post-run review (2026-11-01)

Three reviewers (statistics and verdicts, fault attribution, code and
decision), each followed by a skeptical verifier that re-derived the figures
from the raw files. No verdict changed. What changed in this record:

- **The mechanism of the faults** (confirmed by its verifier and by a separate
  check against the GGUF's tensor offsets). The first draft said the load's prefetch leaves the file in standby,
  from which the balloon's pressure takes pages. The per-layer pattern above
  shows two sources instead: the file's tail never stayed resident, and the
  head is lost in file order as the run's own memory grows; the balloon only
  sets the budget. In-hand data (the three copy speeds, the probe's kept ranges)
  supports the standby and working-set part, which the draft had called
  untested.
- **Windows for every share**: the first draft set `pg`'s 31% (tokens 3-126)
  beside 3-1022 shares; at matched windows `pg` is about 3 points below `prof`.
  The composition explanation covers 103% over tokens 3-1022 and over-explains
  (126%) over 387-1022.
- **Memory pressure**: the per-run minima are mostly load-phase samples; the
  decode-phase minima above are matched within each pinning, and the floor
  check cannot see 2 to 5 s of the load.
- **H3**: the fast-copy idle that rose with the prefetch is now reported next
  to the pre-registered ratio.
- **Smaller corrections**: the prediction for `prof-pq` missed (outside its
  range, not "beat" it); the prefetch-cost breakdown now includes the blocks
  with no candidate; mode 1's cost is a walk over resident pages, not reads.
- **Code**: the `LLAMA_MOE_PREFETCH` comment was split mid-sentence and did not
  say that mode 2 is mode 1 off Windows; reworded after the runs (comment only,
  no code change). Not changed, and noted in Limits or ROADMAP R12b: no warning
  when `QueryWorkingSetEx` fails, candidates in pinned host buffers, the copy
  log kept in memory.

A second check of every figure in the edited docs (320 checked, each
discrepancy re-derived by a verifier) corrected 24: unit slips (MiB read as
thousandths of a GiB), gate G's rates and per-page costs given for k = 1 only,
prefill counts from mismatched cuts, "layers 0-9" where only 0-4 were slow
at the prefill, the GPU-uploaded share of the non-expert tensors (2.0, not
2.49 GiB), the claim that Linux would unmap them (only the file outside the
CPU-mapped span is unmapped there), absolute wording ("every", "does not")
where the data says 99.9%, and statements about inherited pinning that were
only likely. Three were refuted.

Not confirmed or not acted on: the per-token bootstrap of `prof-pf`'s margin
(a within-run interval says little about the two-round rule; the per-round
values are in the pairs table), and the slow re-reads as "just under the
threshold" (one at 0.70 GB/s is plausibly a real fault).
