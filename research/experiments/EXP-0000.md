## Hypotheses tested

Three, over two days:

1. **H1** - the upstream SLRU expert cache thrashes below one token's experts, and
   a frequency-aware victim recovers a large share of the lost hit rate. **The
   main hypothesis.**
2. **H2** - registering the mmap'd host expert pages as pinned memory removes the
   driver staging copy and raises effective host-to-device bandwidth.
3. **H3** - a previous-token expert prefetch would pay for its bandwidth.
   Dropped before implementation on trace evidence.

## Motivation

The upstream cache (`src/llama-moe-cache.cpp`, `--moe-cache-mib`) already existed,
as did seven relevant in-flight upstream PRs, all checked before any code was
written. The one unexplored lever was the **replacement policy at small cache
sizes**, which upstream PR #30848 explicitly leaves alone ("performance may
suffer"). On an 8 GiB card with a 20.81 GiB model, small cache sizes are the only
sizes available, so that gap is exactly the case this machine lives in.

See SOURCES.md for the upstream survey.

## Results

### H1 - sampled least-used victim: confirmed

Base `d854d98b`, BENCHMARKS.md B1, 255 greedy tokens:

| cache MiB | slots/token | upstream hit | branch hit | traffic |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 384 | 0.66 | 0.00% | **21.32%** | **-21.3%** |
| 528 | 0.90 | 1.04% | **27.53%** | **-26.7%** |
| 586 | 1.00 | 20.93% | **29.64%** | **-11.0%** |
| 645 | 1.10 | 29.96% | 29.96% | 0% |
| 1024 | 1.75 | 41.36% | 41.36% | 0% |
| 2048 | 3.49 | 61.38% | 61.38% | 0% |

The last three rows came out **byte-identical** between the arms, to the
hundredth of a MiB - the designed inertness above the crossover, measured.

Throughput, B2, order-balanced over two passes: +32.2%, +47.1%, +25.8% below the
crossover; +0.7%, +3.7%, -3.9% on the three control rows. The control rows are
the noise floor of that session, so the sub-crossover gains are far outside it.

Session A agreed: hit rates within 0.8 points, throughput +17.0%, +32.9%, +17.1%
with a +-1.3% noise floor.

**Session A's absolute throughput is 10 to 15% higher at every size, and its
ratios are smaller.** Host memory pressure in session B penalises whichever arm
reads more bytes - the upstream arm - inflating the ratio. The gain is real; its
size is a property of machine load.

### H2 - host page pinning: real mechanism, confounded measurement

Up to +88% at 81% pin coverage, but locking pages both removes the staging copy
**and** stops this machine's mmap page-cache misses against an 18.32 GiB working
set. Low coverage is mildly counterproductive (-9% at 10%). Spread +-25 to 45%,
the widest measurement in the project.

Table in KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md Finding 4.

### H3 - previous-token prefetch: rejected before implementation

Token-to-token expert overlap within a layer is only 31 to 37%, and 19 to 23% at
a lag of 8 tokens - below what the hot set already delivers. A prefetch would
spend bandwidth to do worse than the cache it feeds. FAILED_APPROACHES.md.

### Supporting measurements

- **Routing skew**: hottest 8 of 256 experts take 31 to 33% of a layer's
  selections, hottest 64 take 83 to 85%. This is the mechanism behind H1.
- **Effective host-to-device rate**: 3.4 to 5.5 GB/s against ~14 GB/s of usable
  PCIe 4.0 x8, near-constant across a 2.6x range of transferred bytes. Generation
  is bandwidth-bound on the copy, which is why bytes convert near-linearly into
  tokens per second. Derived, not a bus measurement.
- **Prefill**: a 54-token prompt pushed 5696.85 MiB (session A) / 5707.74 MiB
  (session B) with zero cache reuse.
- **Belady**: a large gap remains everywhere (branch 15.8 to 32.0% against
  31.9 to 53.9% below the crossover). Simulated.

## Statistical variation

- Hit rate and uploaded bytes are **exact** for a fixed model, prompt and greedy
  decode, and reproduced across the rebase within 0.8 points.
- Throughput noise floor is read off the control rows: +-1.3% at ~18 GiB free,
  +3.7% to -3.9% at 16.1 GiB free.
- The pinning sweep is +-25 to 45%, except the 81% configuration which measured
  17.59 and 17.47 across two passes.
- Simulator vs hardware agreement: within 0.7 to 0.8 points, the residual being
  different RNG streams (C++ LCG vs Python).

## Correctness

BENCHMARKS.md B3. All configurations byte-identical to `--moe-cache-mib 0` on
both bases: policy on and off, above and below the crossover, host page pinning,
and (session A only) upstream master with and without the cache.

## Conclusions

- **H1: Adopt.** Reproducible on two bases, correct, and inert above the
  crossover by construction and by measurement. The threshold reuses a condition
  upstream already detects, so no tuning constant is added.
- **H2: Inconclusive.** The mechanism is real and documented, but the measured
  gain cannot be separated from page residency on this host. Needs R4.
- **H3: Reject**, on trace evidence, before implementation.

## Limitations

1. **One model, one GPU.** 256 experts, 8 used; no second MoE architecture, no
   multi-device split, no second quantization.
2. **Timing is sensitive to host memory pressure**, which is why hit rate and
   uploaded bytes carry the claims.
3. **Pinning is permanently confounded** on this host.
4. **`--load-mode none` was never benchmarked** against capped pinning.
5. **PCIe width was never varied**; the bandwidth axis is approximated by cache
   size.
6. **Upstream's router changed between the two bases**, so session A's simulated
   tables cannot be quoted for `d854d98b`.
7. **No raw results were preserved.**
