# EXP-0004 - Is the expert upload overlapped with compute? A per-token timeline

**Status: complete. Decision: Adopt (measurement and tooling; no code change,
no performance claim).** Iteration 4, authorized 2026-10-31. Settles
OPEN_QUESTIONS.md **Q6**, settles part of **Q3**, and reorders the roadmap.

What it found, in one paragraph: **the expert uploads never overlap compute** -
0 ns in every token of 15 traced runs, and the code explains why (one stream,
two host waits per MoE layer). Finding 4's "3.4 to 5.5 GB/s" was bytes divided
by whole-token time, not a link rate; the copies themselves run at 8.2 to 13 GB/s
pageable and 12.8 GB/s pinned. In a short fresh process **most of a token is GPU
idle while the host thread waits on hard page faults** of the mmap'd expert pages
(91.7 of 139.6 ms at 2048 MiB). The faults die away over about 400 tokens; warm,
a 2048 MiB token is **56.2 ms = 12.8 compute + 27.1 DMA + 0.1 read-back + 16.1
idle** (tokens 387 to 1022). Perfect overlap could hide at most **9.4 ms of that
(17%)**; pinning all experts could reach about **35 ms (+63% tokens per
second)**, but this host cannot pin that much.

## Hypothesis

Stated as STATE.md framed the question before the iteration, which is the
position Finding 4 took:

**H1** - the expert uploads overlap GPU compute, so time per token is close to
`max(compute, bytes / link rate)` and Finding 4's derived 3.4 to 5.5 GB/s is a
lower bound on the link rate.

**Falsified by** measured copy/compute overlap near zero, or by time per token
well above `max(compute, DMA time)`.

**H2** - generation is bandwidth-bound on the link: DMA is the largest part of
a token.

**Falsified by** another component being larger.

**Outcome: H1 falsified on both counts. H2 holds only once warm**, and even then
the DMA runs well below the link rate because the host's staging of the next
pageable copy slows it. In a short fresh process, page faults dominate.

## Motivation

Three iterations (EXP-0001 to 0003) cut bytes on Finding 4's premise that the
copy is the bottleneck and that compute hides behind it. That premise was never
measured. If the copy and compute are serialized, the lever is scheduling rather
than bytes, and the roadmap reorders. STATE.md proposed a cheap indirect test
(`max(compute, bytes/bw)` against token time); Nsight Systems turned out to be
installed, so the direct test - a CUDA timeline of every generated token - was
as cheap and needed no source change.

## Method

**Token boundaries.** Each generated token ends with one 993,280-byte logits
read-back (248,320 floats); the interval between two read-backs is one token.
Decode tokens 1 and 2 are excluded (449 and 693 ms at 2048 MiB: token 1 runs
eagerly and token 2 captures the graphs, but most of their time is page-fault
idle, 320 and 605 ms), so "128-token runs" report tokens 3 to 126.

**Per token**, from the CUPTI tables: **compute** is the union of CUDA graph spans
and individual kernels; **DMA** the union of host-to-device copies; **idle** what
is left of the token after every GPU activity. Each idle interval is attributed
to the host API call the main thread was inside at the time:
`cudaStreamSynchronize`, `cudaMemcpyAsync`, `cudaGraphLaunch`, or no call at all.

**Slow and fast copies.** An expert copy (>= 512 KiB; the smallest expert tensor
is 576 KiB) is **slow** if its host call ran below 1.5 GB/s. The host-rate
distribution is bimodal: only 4 of 45,441 copies fall between 1 and 2 GB/s, and
moving the threshold anywhere in that range changes slow counts by at most 1.7%.

**Run order.** `bench_timeline.sh` ran every arm traced (t1), then untraced (u),
then traced again (t2), in the order 256, 586, 1024, 2048 pageable, 1024, 2048
pinned. That order turned out to matter (below).

**Code reading.** Three readers traced one generated token through the scheduler,
the cache and the CUDA backend; six adversarial verifiers checked every claim
against the source (`code_reading.json`). The result is ARCHITECTURE.md "One
generated token, in order".

**Literature.** A survey of overlap and prefetch work for offloaded MoE decode,
with a second reader checking every citation (`literature.json`, SOURCES.md).

**Adversarial review.** Four skeptics (measurement, page faults, arithmetic,
implications) attacked twelve draft claims; a synthesis resolved their
disagreements against the data (`review.json`). One claim was refuted (C10) and
part of another (C5); eight were corrected in all and three confirmed with
wording fixes. **Every figure in this record is the post-review figure.** A
second pass then checked every figure in the written documents against the raw
results; all 54 of its findings (errors, overstatements, some repeated across
files) were applied (`doc_check.json`). A third pass confirmed those fixes and
found 18 more, mostly stale pre-EXP-0004 text, also applied
(`doc_recheck.json`).

## Results

### H1 falsified: uploads never overlap compute

**Overlap is 0 ns in every token of all 15 captures** (12 short, 3 long),
prefill included. Level 1, and level 2 from the code: expert copies, CUDA graphs
and kernels all run on one stream, and per MoE layer the sequence is graph
launch, sync, 32-byte router read-back, sync, expert copies, next graph: two
syncs per MoE layer, 80 per token. With the syncs around small per-token input
copies and the logits read-back that is **109 `cudaStreamSynchronize` per
token** (114 at 256 and 586 MiB). About ten
small pinned copies per token (<= 8 KiB) go on a second stream and do not overlap
compute either.

**Time per token is far above `max(compute, DMA)`.** At 2048 MiB pageable, cold:
139.6 ms against `max(12.9, 26.3)`. Warm: 56.2 against `max(12.8, 27.1)`.

**GPU compute is 12.7 to 12.9 ms per token in every arm and window**,
independent of cache size and pinning. CUDA graphs are used: 41 graph launches
and no individual kernels in every analysed short-run token. In the 1024-token
runs, every 256th token (211, 467, 723, 979) re-captures and runs 37 of the 41
graphs eagerly. Those tokens' compute is 0.2 to 0.3 ms above a graph token in
11 of 12 cases (1.7 ms in one) and never below, so graph spans are a fair
measure of compute.

call plus small copies and graph launches. Hit rates: 16.53%, 30.74%, 40.17%,

**In a short fresh process, 55 to 66% of the mean token in the pageable arms
(586 to 2048 MiB) is the GPU waiting for the host to fault expert pages in from
disk**; 49 to 57% with 42% pinned.

The 256 MiB row does not fit and is used for no conclusion. Its second rep and
its untraced run followed pinned runs and prefilled partly warm (7.0 s against
13.6 s), yet the cold first rep made fewer slow copies (25.1 per token, 136.0 ms)
than the warm second rep (37.2, 152.3 ms), and 1636 copy positions are slow in
the second rep only. Its faults are not pure first touches; why is not known.

At 256 and 586 MiB one layer gets no cache slots ("budget is too small for 1
layers") and runs its MoE on the CPU. That time lands in "other" (2.8 to 3.1 ms
of idle outside any CUDA call, against 0.7 to 0.9 in the other arms) and adds
five syncs per token.

### The slow copies are hard page faults

Level 1, from four independent signs:

1. **Host rate.** The slow peak is 0.32 to 0.50 GB/s, about **175 us per 64 KiB
   cluster** (p10 150, p90 203 us), read synchronously one cluster at a time.
2. **Disk bytes match.** Over the decode window of the three 1024-token runs
   (the only ones with paging counters), aligned to the trace (first to last
   logits read-back), slow-copy bytes against disk read bytes:

   | arm | slow-copy bytes | disk reads | per-second correlation |
   |---|---:|---:|---:|
   | 2048 pageable | 6.18 GiB | 6.29 GiB | 0.999 |
   | 2048 pinned 42% | 4.82 GiB | 5.00 GiB | 0.999 |
   | 586 pageable | 4.57 GiB | 5.67 GiB | 0.855 |

   Pages Input x 4 KiB equals disk reads (6.28 vs 6.29 GiB), so every decode
   read is a paging read. At 586 MiB about 19% of the reads fall outside slow
   copies, most of them in partly faulted copies that run at 1.5 to 4 GB/s and
   so count as fast; about 0.5 GiB is unexplained.
3. **Deterministic, then decaying.** Slow copies fall at the same positions in
   both reps (6819 and 6819 at 2048 MiB), except at 256 MiB. They fall from 40.8
   per token in the first 128 tokens to 2.2 in tokens 771 to 898. That fits first
   touch, but the trace does not say which tensor each copy was, so re-faults
   after the OS evicts a page are not excluded.
4. **Pinning removes its share.** Pinned arms fault 59.7% (2048) and 61.1%
   (1024) as often in cold runs, where 43.5 to 44.6% of the uploaded bytes come
   from pinned memory, so 55 to 57% of the traffic can still fault (49 to 50%
   pinned in the warm windows). Only 1 of about 296,500 pinned expert copies
   was slow.

The review ruled out staging-buffer exhaustion, CPU contention, WDDM paging and
memory compression as alternative causes.

**Run order confounds this experiment.** Every pageable run that followed a
pinned run started partly warm: it prefilled in 3.7 to 7.3 s instead of 13.4 to
13.8 s. The 2048 MiB pageable long run made 41.6 slow copies per token in tokens
3 to 126 against 55.0 cold, and the 586 MiB long run 21.5 against 53.9 (112.7
against 164.3 ms/token). So the OS **does** keep part of the model cached after a
pinned run. Pinned runs start the same whatever ran before (32.80 against 32.84
slow copies per token). Whole-run disk reads were 15.9, 21.3 and 25.0 GiB for
2048 pageable, 2048 pinned and 586 pageable, including llama.cpp's whole-file
prefetch at load.

### DMA rates, and what Finding 4 actually measured

- **Pageable DMA runs at 12.9 to 13.1 GB/s when the host is not staging the next
  pageable copy, and 8.2 GB/s when it is** (more than 50% overlap). The split
  holds within every copy-size class (576, 704, 840 KiB) and for the pageable
  copies of the pinned runs (8.2 against 13.0). At 2048 MiB pageable, 21.7 of
  the 26.3 ms of DMA per token is contended. Aggregate fast-copy rates are 8.5 to
  9.0 GB/s on the GPU side and 7.1 to 7.6 GB/s on the host side. Whether the
  cause is host memory bandwidth or the driver's staging path is untested.
- **Pinned copies run at 12.8 GB/s** (13.1 for 1 to 1.25 MiB copies, about
  0.8 us gaps) and return to the host at once (128 to 141 GB/s apparent). That
  is **81% of the 15.75 GB/s raw PCIe 4.0 x8 rate**. The "~14 GB/s usable"
  figure used in earlier documents was never measured.
- **Finding 4's 3.4 to 5.5 GB/s divided uploaded bytes by whole-token time**,
  which includes compute, faults, staging and syncs. It is an effective rate,
  not a link rate. It is still a lower bound on the copy rate, but a loose one,
  and the premise that made it look tight - compute hiding behind the copies -
  was false.

### Pinning's gain, split (Q3 in part)

| regime | pinning 42% saves | slow-copy idle | DMA | fast staging |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| cold, 2048 MiB (short runs, both cold) | 42.1 ms/token | 36.3 | 3.7 | 2.0 |
| cold, 1024 MiB | 44.6 | 35.0 | 5.6 | 3.8 |
| warm, 2048 MiB (tokens 387-1022) | 9.6 | 2.8 | 5.4 | 1.5 |

**Cold, 78 to 86% of the gain is fault avoidance. Warm, most of it is the DMA
rate**, which by the contention result above is the removal of staging
contention, not a faster link. The 1024-token first window shows only -23.5 ms
because the pageable long run started partly warm. The cold rows are means of
two 128-token reps per arm; the warm row is one 1024-token rep per arm.

The default cap pins layers 0 to 15 and the gate and down tensors of layer 16
(address order, first fit per tensor; the expert tensors are separate ranges
that never merge). They carry about 49% of the warm upload bytes (layers 0 to 15
alone 48%) for 42% of the expert bytes, because the lowest layers are the least
cacheable (Finding 8).

### Bounds on the levers

Warm 2048 MiB pageable, per layer, from `overlap_bound.py` (level 1 inputs; the
bounds are arithmetic, level 4). Figures are for tokens 387 to 1022 (56.2 ms),
with the best window, tokens 771 to 898 (52.1 ms), in brackets where that is the
one that was computed.

- **Overlap can hide at most 9.4 ms per token (17%)** [9.3 of 52.1, 18%] with a
  perfect one-layer-ahead prediction and an ideal copy engine, and **10.4** with
  unlimited lookahead inside the token. Not the 12.8 of compute: split `S(39)`,
  which ends in the LM head, takes 2118 us after the token's last upload, 3.3 of
  40 layers per token upload nothing, and the typical split (270 us) is shorter
  than the median upload (654 us), so compute is the binding side in 74% of
  layer visits. At the pinned rate the bound is 9.0 ms; at 70 to 90% recall it
  is still 8.8 to 9.1 ms [computed on 771 to 898].
- **Pinning every expert** would cut DMA to about 27.1 x 8.5 / 12.8 = 18.0 ms
  and remove faults and staging: **about 34.6 ms per token (+63% tokens per
  second)** [33.9 on 771 to 898; paired per-layer data give 34.4]. **This host
  cannot do it**: Windows charges pinned pages to a budget of about half the RAM
  (`src/llama-moe-cache.cpp`, about line 701), and the earlier sweep registered
  at most 15266 MiB (81%). In address order that cap leaves layer 32's up tensor
  and layers 33 to 39 pageable, about 18% of the warm upload bytes, and the
  optimistic projection is **about 38 ms** [on 771 to 898, +37%], a little more
  on 387 to 1022. Leaving the least-missed layers pageable instead would leave
  about 13 to 14% of the traffic pageable.
- **The per-layer host round trip** (sync, read-back, plan, the pageable
  slot-map copy, graph launch) is 65 to 86 us, **2.6 to 3.4 ms per token**: 5%
  now, about 10% of a pinned token. `cudaStreamSynchronize` alone is 1.9 to
  2.2 ms per token.

**The levers compound.** Pinning first and then adding a copy stream with
next-layer prediction would move a warm token from about 35 toward 25 ms (pinned
DMA plus one-layer-ahead overlap gives about 25.6), and overlap's share grows
after pinning, to about 26%. The two closest measurements of prefetch alone
(ProMoE 1.09 to 1.17x inside llama.cpp, Speculating Experts 9 to 14% on
Qwen3-30B-A3B), 8 to 15% of token time, are about half to most of the 17 to 18%
overlap ceiling; the wider literature reports +5 to +34% tokens per second,
about 5 to 25% of token time (SOURCES.md).

### Trace completeness and tracing overhead

**No copy was dropped.** Trace-counted expert upload bytes over decode equal the
cache's own counter minus exactly 15.5625 MiB in every arm: 24 copies (16 x
589,824 B gate/up + 8 x 860,160 B down) of 8 experts of the last MoE layer,
uploaded through the cache during the prefill because output pruning leaves that
layer's FFN with one token. nsys event counts are exact, there are no drop
diagnostics, and correlation ids are unique.

**Tracing costs +0.6 to +2.2% per token** (app eval time, ABA order):

| arm | traced against untraced | ms/token |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1024 pageable | +2.2% | +3.5 |
| 586 pageable | +1.9% | +3.2 |
| 2048 pageable | +1.1% | +1.5 |
| 2048 pinned | +0.8% | +0.8 |
| 1024 pinned | +0.6% | +0.6 |

The 256 MiB arm (-0.4%; reps -4.6% and +3.7%) is unusable for this, for the run
order reason above. The overhead, about 2.4 us per API call, is host-side and
lands in idle, so the small idle buckets (sync 2.2, other 1.6) carry about
+-1 ms; compute, DMA and slow-copy idle are unaffected.

### Calibration against B2

Warm 2048 MiB here (52 to 56 ms, traced) is **10 to 36% slower per token than
the B2 llama-bench references** at 2048 MiB (41.2 to 47.1 ms on older bases). At 586 MiB
the two agree (B2 75 to 79 ms against 74.0 to 77.7). The gap is unexplained;
candidates are llama-bench's warm-up and repeated runs in one process, tracing,
and the base. Reconciling it is part of next step 0.

### Incidental

- **llama.cpp prefetches the whole 20.8 GiB file at load**
  (`src/llama-mmap.cpp`, about lines 611-627), more than the free RAM, and it does
  not keep the experts resident. The startup prefetch reads at 3 to 4 GB/s on the
  same disk where a decode fault reads at 0.37 GB/s.
- **The MoE cache never prefetches.** `llama_prefetch` (`src/llama-mmap.cpp:837`)
  and `llama_prefetch_rows` (`src/llama-impl.cpp:22`) exist and have no MoE-cache
  caller.
- **Registering 7842 MiB took about 3 ms**, so registration does not fault the
  pages in. Yet only 1 of about 296,500 pinned expert copies in the five pinned
  captures was slow (576 KiB in 5.2 ms, 2048 MiB pinned rep 2, decode token 66;
  prefill included); when those pages become resident is not measured.
- **Partial pinning slows the unpinned layers a little**: 194.7 against 181.7
  us/MiB, one run each. That matches the -9% at 10% pinned in Finding 4.
- nsys logs `driver 13.5 not supported, using 13.2 libraries` as a diagnostic.
  The trace is still complete (above). The CPU-GPU clock offset lies in
  [-1.5, +5.6] us; shifting by it changes the 2048 MiB pageable idle buckets by
  at most 0.11 ms per token.

## Conclusions

**Decision: Adopt (measurement and tooling).**

1. **Q6 is settled: nothing overlaps.** The question STATE.md called the
   highest-value one is answered with a level 1 measurement and a level 2
   explanation.
2. **Finding 4 was wrong in its premise.** The copy is not hidden behind compute,
   and its GB/s were not link rates. It is rewritten in KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md.
3. **Bytes still matter.** Warm, DMA is the largest component, and the policy
   work of EXP-0000 to 0003 cut exactly that. But the rate of each byte and the
   host's waits are as large a lever as the count.
4. **The host's memory is the first lever on this machine**, before scheduling:
   faults and staging contention cost more than overlap could ever hide.
5. **Short single-process runs measure page faults, not steady state.** A cold
   128-token CLI generation is 2.1 to 2.5x slower per token than tokens 387 to
   1022 of the same configuration (2.5x at 2048 MiB pageable), and up to 2.7x
   against the best window.

### Limitations

- One host, one model, one GPU, WDDM, one copy engine.
- The long runs have one traced rep per arm and no untraced warm control.
- **Page-cache state was not controlled** between runs (flushing the standby
  list needs an admin tool), which confounds the 256 MiB arm and the long
  pageable runs.
- `graph:host-only` gives no kernel times inside a graph, so how much a layer
  could overlap **without** prediction (shared expert, hits, gate/up while down
  uploads) is unmeasured.
- The trace does not record which expert each copy carried, so late faults
  cannot be told apart from re-faults after the OS dropped a page.
- "Idle" is idle for this process; another process could have used the GPU.
