# EXP-0002 - Score the victim by distance to the layer's next visit

**Status: complete. Decision: Adopt.** Iteration 2, authorized 2026-10-26.

Roadmap item **R2**, candidate 2 ("frequency plus position in the sweep"), chosen
over candidate 1 by the Q2 per-layer analysis below rather than by guess.

## Hypothesis

**H1** - below one token's experts, weighting the sampled victim's use count by
the *known* cyclic distance to its layer's next visit beats use count alone.

The sweep over a group's layers is cyclic during generation, so the next visit to
a layer is not predicted, it is known: a layer visited `ago` calls back comes
again in `m - ago` calls, where `m` is the number of layers in the group. Belady
evicts the entry whose next use is furthest away. Approximating the time to the
next use of expert `e` of layer `v` as

```
dist(v) / P(e picked when v is visited)
```

and taking the use counter as a proxy for `P` gives a score that should be
strictly better informed than the use counter alone. The layer the sweep has just
passed is the furthest from its next use; the layer just ahead of the sweep is
the nearest - and that is exactly what plain LRU evicts first, which is the
documented thrash mechanism.

**Falsified by** any cache size at or below the crossover where the paired
per-trace delta against `ramp-s16-age8` does not have the same sign on all four
traces (BENCHMARKS.md screening rule).

**H2** - the change stays inert at and above the crossover, so the arms there
remain byte-identical.

**Falsified by** any difference in hits, misses or uploaded bytes at 645, 1024 or
2048 MiB between the new default and `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_SAMPLE=0`.

## Motivation and the analysis that chose the candidate

ROADMAP R2 offered three candidates and OPEN_QUESTIONS.md Q2 was scheduled first
to decide between them: is the remaining Belady gap concentrated in a few layers
(favouring per-layer slot budgets) or spread evenly (favouring a better victim
score)?

`research/results/experiments/EXP-0002/analyze_layers.py` answers it from the
EXP-0001 traces, at 528 MiB, mean of four traces. Full table in
`per_layer_528.json`.

**The gap is close to uniform, so per-layer budgets are the wrong lever:**

| quantity | min | median | max | stdev |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| gap to Belady | 14.49 | 21.14 | 27.50 | **3.23** |
| shipping hit rate | 1.84 | 33.00 | 49.04 | **10.69** |
| top64 share | 54.40 | 86.19 | 94.99 | 8.72 |

The worst 8 of 39 cached layers hold **24.7%** of the total gap where a uniform
spread would give 20.5%. That is barely concentrated at all. Meanwhile the
**hit rate** varies hugely (1.84% to 49.04%) and is almost perfectly explained by
routing skew:

| pair | Pearson r |
|---|---:|
| top8 share vs shipping hit rate | **+0.979** |
| top64 share vs shipping hit rate | +0.944 |
| distinct experts vs shipping hit rate | **-0.924** |
| top64 share vs gap to Belady | -0.218 |

So layers differ enormously in how cacheable they are, and the policy already
tracks that almost perfectly - a flat layer's experts have low use counts and are
already evicted preferentially, which is why `r = +0.979`. What the policy does
**not** exploit is that the gap to Belady is the same ~21 points everywhere
regardless of skew, which says the loss is a *timing* loss shared by all layers,
not an *allocation* loss concentrated in some. Candidate 2, not candidate 1.

Two incidental facts worth recording:

- **Layer 1 is absent from the per-layer table.** It is a one-layer cache group
  (2.195 MiB experts) and at 528 MiB it cannot afford `n_expert_used` slots, so
  it is not cached at all. This is the "a group can get zero slots" hazard from
  ARCHITECTURE.md, seen in the data.
- **The earliest layers are nearly uncacheable.** Layer 0 routes over 236 of 256
  experts with only 13.3% from its hottest 8, and gets 1.84%; layer 2 is similar.
  Layer 20, at the other end, takes 95.0% from its hottest 64 and gets 49.04%.

## Code changes

`src/llama-moe-cache.cpp`, inside the existing `moe_cache_slru`:

- three new members: `n_group_layers`, `n_calls`, and `last_call` (one `int64_t`
  per layer, 320 bytes for this model);
- `plan()` records `last_call[il] = ++n_calls`;
- `dist(il)` returns the calls until the sweep returns to `il`, clamped to at
  least 1, and returns 1 when the term is off or the group has a single layer;
- `victim_sampled()` now maximises `dist/use` instead of minimising `use`,
  compared as a cross-multiplied ratio to stay in integers.

`init()` takes the group's layer count. Everything else is unchanged: the
crossover switch, the aging, the sample count of 16, the "cannot evict what this
request needs" rule, and the fall back to the upstream LRU walk.

`LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_DIST=0` scores by use count only, which reproduces the EXP-0000
policy for A/B measurement - the same pattern as the existing
`LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_SAMPLE`. Implemented by passing a group layer count of 0, so
`dist()` returns a constant and the score reduces to `1/use`.

`moe-bench/moe_sim.py` gains the matching `SlruSampleDistCache` and the
`ramp-s{8,16,32}-age8-dist` policies, plus per-layer hit accounting in
`simulate()`. `moe-bench/check-correctness.sh` gains a `cache528-dist0` case and
a `cache256-auto` case to cover both new paths.

### Throughput: inconclusive, and the control row says why

BENCHMARKS.md B2 at 528 MiB (active) and 1024 MiB (control), order-balanced over
two passes, `-n 64 -p 0 -r 4`. Raw data in `b2_raw.md`.

| MiB | EXP-0000 tok/s | this tok/s | change | |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 528 | 9.07 | 8.22 | -9.4% | change active |
| 1024 | 14.11 | 13.13 | **-6.9%** | **control, arms byte-identical** |

**The control row moved 6.9% on code that B1 proves is byte-identical**, so this
measurement cannot separate the arms, and the 528 row's -9.4% is not evidence of
a regression any more than it would have been evidence of a gain. Per-run spread
was +-1.3 to +-2.7 tok/s on means of 8 to 15, which is +-16 to 27%, the widest
of any throughput measurement in this project.

Free host RAM was about 17 GiB at the start and 17.63 GiB afterwards, better than
EXP-0001's 12.34, but the machine was not otherwise quiet. Absolute throughput is
also below EXP-0000 session B at the same sizes (9.07 against 10.65 at 528 MiB
for the same policy), which is the same host-load effect.

This was the expected outcome and was predicted before the run: a 2.4% traffic
reduction converts to roughly 2 to 3% of throughput at the measured near-linear
rate, and that is far inside the noise floor. **No throughput claim is made in
either direction.** The exact metrics carry the result, which is the project's
established methodology.

The one theoretical cost worth naming: `victim_sampled` now does two 64-bit
multiplies per sampled slot. At 528 MiB that is about 16 samples x 56k fills =
1.8M extra multiplies over 255 tokens, against roughly 100 GiB of PCIe traffic in
the same window. Too small to measure here, and nothing in the data suggests
otherwise.

## Conclusions

**Decision: Adopt.**

- **H1 confirmed.** The distance-weighted victim beats the EXP-0000 policy by
  **+1.74 to +2.94 points** of hit rate on hardware and **+1.06 to +2.73** in the
  simulator, sign-consistent on all four traces at all eight simulated sizes.
  PCIe traffic falls a further **2.3 to 3.4%** on top of EXP-0000's reduction.
- **H2 confirmed.** Byte-identical above the crossover on hardware at three
  sizes.
- Correctness gate passed, 8 of 8.
- The change is **49 lines** in `src/llama-moe-cache.cpp`, inside the function it
  already modified, and adds **no new tuning constant** - the cyclic distance is
  read from the group's layer count, which the cache already knows.

Against upstream the combined effect below the crossover is now **+16.3 to
+28.5% less PCIe traffic** and a hit rate of 16.3 to 31.4% where upstream returns
0.00 to 20.93%.

### Why the switch was kept despite the distance term helping above the crossover

The distance term turns the EXP-0000 policy's clear loss above the crossover
(-1.75 to -3.08 points) into a wash (+0.42 and +0.35 at 645 and 1024 MiB, then
-1.43 and -1.17 at 2048 and 3072). Tempting, but the screening rule from EXP-0001
requires the sign to agree on all four traces and it does not at any of those
sizes. Keeping the switch also preserves the byte-identical control rows that
every throughput claim in this project is read against. Recorded as a question
rather than a change: OPEN_QUESTIONS.md Q10.

### Limitations

1. **Throughput unmeasured**, as above. The claim is hit rate and bytes only.
2. **One model, one GPU, four prompts.** Unchanged from every prior experiment.
   The mechanism depends on the layer sweep being cyclic, which is a property of
   generation; it does nothing for prefill, which bypasses the cache anyway.
3. **`n_sample` left at 16.** `dist s32` is a further sign-consistent +0.39 to
   +0.87 below the crossover. Deliberately not taken here to keep one hypothesis
   per change; it is now a ROADMAP item.
4. **The distance estimate assumes a cyclic sweep.** During prefill, or any
   non-cyclic access order, `dist` degrades to an arbitrary but bounded weight
   rather than becoming wrong - the fall back to the LRU walk and the "cannot
   evict what this request needs" rule are untouched, so correctness does not
   depend on the assumption. Only the quality of the choice does.
5. **Belady is still far ahead**: 32.01% against 18.68% at 256 MiB. This closes
   roughly a sixth of the remaining gap, not the half the hypothesis hoped for.
